# Cutting Grammar

Do not default to one continuous take. Use one-take only when continuity, spatial reveal, choreography, or suspense specifically needs it. For dialogue conflict, family pressure, comedy timing, action escalation, and emotional reversals, use cuts.

## Default Decision

- Use hard cuts when a power shift happens.
- Use reaction close-ups when a line wounds someone or changes their expectation.
- Use insert shots for objects that carry story meaning: tea kettle, red bag, wet floor, toy car, door handle, legal document.
- Use over-shoulder shots for confrontation across a boundary; they preserve fixed positions while showing power direction and eyeline.
- Use low-angle/wide shots to re-establish fixed positions after several close-ups.
- Use one-take only when the camera movement itself reveals new information.

## Common Shot Types

- Establishing fixed-position wide: shows the spatial map and power hierarchy.
- Over-shoulder confrontation: keeps both sides connected across a table/door/road; the near shoulder becomes a foreground frame and the speaker/listener remains spatially locked.
- Reaction close-up: catches humiliation, shock, suppressed anger, hope collapsing.
- Insert: isolates an object or small action with narrative weight.
- Cutaway: shows a third party watching, smirking, hiding, or misunderstanding.
- Re-establishing wide: returns after close-ups to prevent spatial drift.
- Door/threshold cut: moves from public conflict to private threat.

## Domestic Drama Cutting Pattern

For a living-room conflict, prefer this structure:

1. Establishing low wide: sofa/table/carpet positions locked.
2. Medium two-shot: the lower-status pair reacts.
3. Hard cut to high-status speaker on sofa.
4. Over-shoulder shot from low-status side toward high-status speaker, keeping the table boundary visible.
5. Insert: object that reinforces insult or power, such as leather sofa, wet carpet, red bag, tea kettle.
6. Reverse over-shoulder from high-status side toward the lower-status pair, making them visually trapped below.
7. Reaction close-up: the person wounded by the line.
8. Re-establishing wide: remind the model who is still high/low and where everyone sits.
9. Hard cut to threshold/bedroom if the scene moves into private conflict.

## Prompt Language

Use explicit edit labels:

```text
HARD CUT 1:
{shot scale, camera height, subject, action, dialogue}

INSERT SHOT:
{object detail and sound}

OVER-SHOULDER SHOT:
保持记忆性固定站位，以{foreground character}的肩膀/后脑边缘作为前景框架，越过{separator object}拍向{target character}，明确双方高低/远近/左右关系。

REVERSE OVER-SHOULDER:
保持同一空间轴线，从对方肩后反打回来，人物不得换边，视线方向必须匹配上一镜。

REACTION CLOSE-UP:
{face/action/breath/eye movement}

RE-ESTABLISHING WIDE:
保持记忆性固定站位，重新展示人物高低关系。
```

## Continuity Rule

After every cut, state whether fixed positions persist or change:

- "同一场景连续剧情严格承接上一段末帧"
- "硬切后仍保持上述固定站位"
- "只改变景别，不改变人物相对位置"
- "镜头切到特写，但角色仍位于茶几前方地毯低位"
- "切回 wide shot 重新确认沙发/茶几/地毯三层关系"
- "过肩反打必须保持同一轴线，不能让人物左右互换"
- "场景切换后先重新建立新场景固定站位，再写镜头"

## Negative Constraints

Add these when cuts are used:

```text
NOT 一镜到底; NOT 连续跟拍; NOT 因硬切导致人物换边; NOT 过肩镜头越轴; NOT 反打时人物左右互换; NOT 插入镜头后丢失茶几/沙发/地毯空间关系; NOT 反打镜头改变人物前后关系。
```
